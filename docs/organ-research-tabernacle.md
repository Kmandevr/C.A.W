# Organ research: the Salt Lake Tabernacle organ

Research for **Bittern**, C.A.W's pipe organ, which the owner wants to be "a genuine organ, not a bunch of instruments bundled together". All sources were read on 2026-11-25; they are listed at the end as [S1]...[S16]. Sections 1-5 are **facts** with their source. Anything marked **Assumption** or **Proposal** is not from a source. Nothing here is built.

## 1. The instrument

**Builders and dates (facts).**
- 1867: Joseph Ridges built the first organ, with 2 manuals [S1]. It was first played at the October 1867 general conference with about 700 pipes, 27 pedals and 12 working stops [S6].
- Rebuilt and enlarged by Niels Johnson (1889), Kimball (1901) and Austin (1916, 1926, 1937) [S1]. Around 1915 the case was widened by about 15 feet on each side, giving its present look [S6]. The 2024 specification names the case builders as Joseph Ridges (1867) and Cannon and Fetzer (1916), in pine [S1].
- The contract with Æolian-Skinner of Boston was signed in 1945. G. Donald Harrison was the firm's president and tonal director [S1]. The organ is **Opus 1075, dated 1948** [S1]. It was "essentially new", though some older pipework was kept [S1]. Harrison pronounced it complete on 16 Jan 1949 [S7]. The OHS stoplist entry dates it 1949 [S3].
- Additions came in 1979 and in 1984-88. These were "mostly stops which Harrison intended to install but which were omitted" [S1].
- 1984-88: Schoenstein & Co. (San Francisco), under Jack M. Bethards [S8, S2]. Bethards called the work "not another step in the organ's evolution but rather a conclusion of Harrison's 1948 project" [S8]. It added about 15 ranks [S8], among them a Positiv Principal 8', a mounted Cornet and reeds on the Great, a Flûte Harmonique and two high-pressure reeds [S8]. Every pipe was regulated [S8].
- 2005-07: Rocky Mountain Organ Co. did the work during the Tabernacle's seismic renovation. About 70 ranks were removed, all 11,623 pipes were washed, and the pitch was raised to A=440 [S9].
- 2021: new couplers and sequencer controls, plus an IOTI Virtuoso control system [S1].
- May-Aug 2025: a mechanical renovation led by Temple Square's organ technician Joseph Nielsen. The work aims to "revive historic sounds", and "two sets of pipes removed in the 1970s" (string voices) are to be reintroduced [S6]. **Open question:** the 18 April 2024 specification [S1] may not show these two ranks yet.

**Size (facts).** The current specification gives 147 voices, 206 ranks and 11,623 pipes, on 5 manuals and pedal [S1]. The detached console has electro-pneumatic key and stop action [S1]. The OHS database instrument page counts 149 stops and 171 registers, with 5 manuals of 61 notes, a 32-note concave radiating pedalboard and 8 divisions [S2]. Its stoplist page gives 147 stops [S3]. This note uses [S1] where they disagree. The longest pipe speaks at 32 ft and the shortest at ¾ inch [S10].

**The façade (facts).** Of the façade pipes, only the 10 largest speak, and they date from Ridges' original organ. The rest are show pipes [S7]. The 2007 report also calls façade pipes "non-speaking" [S9]. The façade was gilded in the 2004-07 work [S5]. Three Pedal 32' flues are marked as retained from earlier Tabernacle organs: Montre (an extension of the Great Subprincipal), Flûte Ouverte and Contre Bourdon [S1]. **Open question:** no source read says which stop the 10 speaking façade pipes belong to.

**Divisions and wind pressures (facts, [S1]).**

| Division (manual) | Voices / ranks / pipes | Enclosed | Wind |
|---|---|---|---|
| Positiv (I) | 16 / 21 / 1,257 | no | 2⅝" (67 mm) |
| Choir (I) | 18 / 24 / 1,536 | yes | 4¾" (121 mm) |
| Great (II) | 29 / 44 / 2,564 | no | 3½", 3⅞", 4 9/16", 4⅞" (89-124 mm) |

The Antiphonal sits at the east (rear) end of the hall and projects westward [S7]. Tuning is equal temperament, A=440 Hz at 74 °F [S1].

## 3. Sound character

- **American Classic.** The Church calls the organ "a quintessential example of the American Classic style", an eclectic approach that "G. Donald Harrison" brought "to its zenith" [S1]. Tabernacle organist John Longhurst (1984) wrote that the aim was to blend French, English and German practice, so that one organ can play music "from various periods and nations" [S13]. Bethards: nothing should rob the organ of its "characteristic elegance in accompaniment" or its "eminent suitability for radio pickup" [S8].
- **Ensemble philosophy.** These are Harrison's letters, quoted in [S11]:
  - "the finished ensemble is produced by many ranks none of which are loud in themselves" (to Ralph Downes, 14 Jan 1949);
  - "there are no very loud stops, the effect being obtained by the 188 ranks";
  - full organ "is very easy on the ears" (to Henry Willis III, 18 Mar 1949).

  Schreiner (1957) called the foundations, mixtures and reeds "the backbone" and spoke of a "democratic" ensemble in which no stop may "blot out" the weaker voices [S11].
- **Principal chorus and mixtures.** Harrison: "The large-scale Mixtures give quite a powerful resultant effect", "a kind of transparent body" [S11]. Campbell describes Harrison's choruses as built on "the four-foot line", with narrower bass and broader treble scales, low to moderate wind and "gentle but clear articulation" [S11]. The stoplist shows independent choruses with mixtures on the Great (4 mixtures plus a Cornet), Swell, Positiv, Choir and Bombarde, plus a Pedal Grand Harmonics V [S1].
- **Reeds.** Harrison: the ensemble is "less reedy than a French ensemble as the balance between full flues and reeds is entirely different" [S11]. The 1988 work brought "a new pungency in the reeds, which now speak more directly", and high-pressure reeds "to sing out above the choir or the organ when they're going full blast" [S8]. The Swell has a French reed battery (Trompettes, Clairon, Contre Trompette, Quinte Trompette) [S1].
- **Strings and celestes.** Harrison: "The strings are good but not so soul stirring as I had hoped for", which he put down to "a trick of the acoustics" (Dec 1948, [S11]). There are many celestes: Swell Viole and Voix Céleste, Flute Celeste and Orchestral Strings II; Choir Viola Celeste, Dulcet II and Kleine Erzähler II; Solo Gamba Céleste; Antiphonal Voix Céleste [S1]. Two string ranks removed in the 1970s were being restored in 2025 [S6].
- **Flutes.** These include harmonic flutes (Great Flûte Harmonique and Flûte Octaviante, Choir Piccolo Harmonique, Solo Flauto Mirabilis), stopped and chimney flutes (Gedeckt, Cor de Nuit, Koppelflöte) and mutations for cornet colours on the Positiv, Swell and Solo [S1]. Organist Robert Cundick on the Flûte Harmonique: "I don't know how we ever got along without it" [S8].
- **Registrations with the choir.** No source read documents the Tabernacle organists' own choir registrations (searched 2026-11-25). The general guidance from BYU's OrganTutor is that "The PRINCIPAL CHORUS should form the basis for registration in congregational singing", with principal 8' and 4' as "givens" [S14]. The presets in section 6 are therefore **assumptions** built on that guidance and on Harrison's ensemble aims.

## 4. The room

- **Shape (facts, [S4]).** The floor plan is rectangular with semicircular ends. A barrel-vaulted ceiling covers the rectangle, with approximate quarter-spheres over the semicircular ends. "The domed ceiling and the quasi-elliptical footprint of the hall focus sound to different areas"; there are whispering-gallery effects. Volume is 35,300 m³ by direct measurement, with 9,350 m² of absorbing surface (Hales 1930, [S15]).
- **Reverberation, Rollins 2005 (measured just before the 2005-06 seismic renovation) [S4].** These are unoccupied, broadband values from Schroeder-integrated MLS impulse responses with the source at centre stage:

  | Measure | Mean | Range | s.d. |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | T30 | 3.83 s | 2.24-4.57 s | 0.34 s |
  | T10-20 | 3.95 s | not noted | not noted |

  Values fall "mostly between 3.5 and 4.5 seconds". The occupied RT is "about 3 seconds" (Kirkegaard Associates data). The 4 kHz band is shorter, because of high-frequency absorption by the ceiling and the air. Focused reflections from the curved ceiling give a strong spatial spread in EDT and C80: high clarity in the rear balcony and low clarity at the front of the main floor.
- **Hales 1930, JASA [S15].** Hales used Sabine's stopwatch method, with organ pipes as the source. These are times to inaudibility, not ISO RT60.
  - Empty hall: 4.62-4.80 s at middle C, and 4.76 s averaged over 11 seats.
  - All doors and windows open: 5.07 s falling to 4.30 s. With 1,004 people in the balcony: 3.20 s.
  - By pitch: 1.63 s at 16 Hz, a peak of 5.21 s near 348 Hz (F), and 0.97 s at 8,368 Hz.
  - There are flutter echoes on the centre aisle, and up to 14 echoes at four balcony points.
- **What it does to the sound.** Harrison praised the "magnificent acoustics" and "ideal acoustics" [S11]. Rocky Mountain Organ's president said the organ "is complemented by the famous Tabernacle acoustics" [S9]. Rollins notes that negative C80 means more blend than in Boston Symphony Hall at lower frequencies [S4]. **Assumption:** a long, warm, slightly dark tail (mid-band peak, quick high-frequency decay) is what fuses many mild ranks into the "transparent body" Harrison described. No post-2007 measurement was found.

## 5. Recordings and impulse responses under CC0 / CC-BY

- **This organ:** no CC0 or CC-BY recording was found. The Wikimedia Commons category "Salt Lake Tabernacle organ" holds photographs and a stoplist PDF but no audio (API listing, 2026-11-25) [S12]. Freesound returned "No results" for "tabernacle organ", "salt lake organ" and "aeolian skinner" [S16].
- **This room:** Rollins measured hundreds of impulse responses [S4]. No public download of them was found, and the thesis page gives only "http://lib.byu.edu/about/copyright/" as its rights statement, so it is not CC.
- **Similar American organs:** none under CC0 or CC-BY was found. Commercial Hauptwerk sets of Æolian-Skinner organs exist (Sonus Paradisi Op. 1301, Augustine's Op. 1132); these are not free licences and are listed only for orientation. Freesound 64486, "Welte Philharmonic Organ.wav", is marked "Creative Commons 0" [S16]. It is a 1916 Welte organ built with Skinner, now in Speyer: it predates the American Classic and is not a Harrison organ.
- **Similar rooms (OpenAIR, University of York):** the stereo IR sets include **1st Baptist Nashville** (US; 3 positions, 96 kHz), **St George's Episcopal Church**, **York Minster**, **St Paul's Cathedral** and others. The file listing is at webfiles.york.ac.uk/OPENAIR/IRs (read 2026-11-25). Their Read Me files carry no licence. The OpenAIR entry pages returned HTTP 503 on 2026-11-25.
  - A search snippet of the St Patrick's, Patrington page reads "licensed under a Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International License".
  - The audb mirror lists the OpenAIR dataset as "CC-BY-4.0" and gives 1st Baptist Nashville a reverberation time of 2.59.
  - **Licence per entry not verified:** check each entry page before use.

## 6. The model (built 2026-11-25: [engine](engine.md), Bittern)

**Built (2026-11-25; Bittern adds the Swell's Nazard and Tierce, 26 stops):** model one organ: real divisions on separate windchests, a Swell box, couplers and one shared wind and room. Every stop is a real stop from [S1], with its real name and pitch. The cut keeps the Great's principal chorus, the Swell's French reeds and celestes, a Choir accompaniment group, the signature Tuba Mirabilis and a Pedal with both 32's. The 24 stops below come to about 32 ranks (mixtures counted by rank). Rank counts are from [S1]; the cost per rank is an **assumption**.

| Division | Stops (pitch) |
|---|---|
| Great, unenclosed (7) | Subprincipal 16', Principal 8', Flûte Harmonique 8', Octave 4', Super Octave 2', Fourniture IV, Trumpet 8' |
| Swell, enclosed (7) | Geigen Principal 8', Gedeckt 8', Viole Céleste II (Viole de Gambe and Céleste), Flauto Traverso 4', Plein Jeu IV, 1ère Trompette 8', Hautbois 8'; Tremulant |
| Choir, enclosed (3) | Concert Flute 8', Kleine Erzähler II 8', Krummhorn 8'; Tremulant |
| Antiphonal (1) | Tuba Mirabilis 8' (high pressure, placed at the rear in the stereo field) |
| Pedal (6) | Contre Bourdon 32', Principal 16', Bourdon 16', Principal 8', Trombone 16', Bombarde 32' |
| Couplers | Swell to Great, Choir to Great, Great to Pedal, Swell to Pedal |

Not built: the couplers (one keyboard plays everything; [decisions](decisions.md), 2026-11-25) and the Antiphonal's place behind the listener. The Montre 32' is skipped. In the real organ it is an extension of the Great Subprincipal [S1], so a model could add it as an extension at almost no cost (**proposal**).

**Presets (proposal; registrations are assumptions, see section 3):**
1. **Soft accompaniment**: Sw Gedeckt 8' and Viole Céleste II, box mostly closed; Ch Kleine Erzähler II; Ped Bourdon 16' with Sw to Ped.
2. **Hymn plenum**: Gt Principal 8', Octave 4', Super Octave 2', Fourniture IV; Sw Geigen 8' and Plein Jeu IV, Sw to Gt; Ped Principal 16' and 8', Bourdon 16', Gt to Ped. This follows the "principal chorus as basis" guidance [S14].
3. **Hymn with reeds**: as 2, plus Sw 1ère Trompette, Gt Trumpet and Ped Trombone 16'.
4. **Full organ**: everything except the celestes, the Kleine Erzähler, the tremulants and the Tuba Mirabilis; Ped Bombarde 32' and Contre Bourdon 32'.
5. **Tuba over full**: preset 4 on the Great with the Antiphonal Tuba Mirabilis on top, as the high-pressure reeds were added "to sing out above the choir or the organ" [S8].
6. **Harmonic flute solo**: Gt Flûte Harmonique 8' over Sw Viole Céleste II; Ped Bourdon 16'.
7. **Reed solo**: Ch Krummhorn 8' or Sw Hautbois 8' over Sw Gedeckt 8' and Flauto Traverso 4'; Ped Bourdon 16'.
8. **Celestes**: Sw Viole Céleste II and Ch Kleine Erzähler II with the Swell tremulant; Ped Bourdon 16'.

**Room (proposal):** a convolution or algorithmic tail of about 3.8 s unoccupied (about 3 s for an "occupied" variant), shorter at high frequencies, with the Antiphonal placed behind the listener [S4, S7]. No CC-licensed Tabernacle impulse response exists, so it would be synthesised from these figures or taken from a CC-BY OpenAIR church once its licence is checked.
